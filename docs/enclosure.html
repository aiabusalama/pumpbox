Assembly plan · one sealed unit

Pump controller enclosure

Everything on one backplate inside a single IP65 box: contactor, protection gear, power supplies, Pi and screen. Four cables in, one cable out, nothing loose.

  • 143 V array
  • 24 V control
  • 5 V logic
  • data

The box

A 300 × 200 × 150 mm polycarbonate enclosure, IP65, with a clear lid. The contactor alone is 45 × 58 × 57 mm, and it needs air around it, so 150 mm depth is the working minimum — a 100 mm box will not close over the contactor plus wiring.

The clear lid matters: the LCD stays sealed inside and you read it through the cover. No cutout, no gasket to fail, no water path.

Ask for the version with a mounting backplate (montaj plakası). Everything screws to that plate on the bench, then the finished plate drops into the box as one piece. Assembling inside a box is miserable.

Layout on the backplate

Keep the 143 V and 5 V sides apart. Board A carries array voltage; Board B and the Pi do not. Route their wiring on opposite sides of the plate and never bundle them together in the same tie.

Assembly order

  1. Drill the plate, mount the rail

    Lay every component on the bare plate and shuffle until it fits with room to work. Mark the holes, drill, then fit the DIN rail across the top.

    Leave 25 mm clear around the contactor. It dissipates 1.3 W continuously and wants air.

  2. Solder Board A

    Three terminal blocks, the HLK-10M24, and the LM2596 onto one perfboard.

    • Terminals and HLK are PCB-pin parts — they solder through the board
    • Give the LM2596 header sockets so it can be swapped without desoldering
    • Use thicker wire for the 143 V traces; thin hookup wire is not enough

  3. Set the LM2596 — before it touches anything

    Feed it 24 V with the output disconnected. Turn the trimmer until your meter reads 5.1 V. Only then wire the output.

    These ship set anywhere from 1.2 V to 35 V. An unadjusted one destroys the Pi the instant you connect it.

  4. Solder Board B

    MAX485 on header sockets, and header pins for the jumper leads to the Pi. Mount the Pi beside it on M3 standoffs.

  5. Mount both boards on standoffs

    10 mm minimum so solder joints never touch the plate. Longer ones under the LCD bring it close to the lid for easy reading.

  6. Wire inside the box

    Ferrules on every stranded end. A single loose strand across 143 V is a fire. Ring lugs on the shunt bolts.

    Leave a service loop on each run so the boards can be lifted out without disconnecting everything.

  7. Fit glands and test dry

    Glands along the bottom edge only. Run the whole bench test with the lid open before anything sees array voltage.

Already on hand: Pi Zero W, PZEM-017 with shunt, 20 × 4 LCD, 5 V relay, HLK-10M24, HLK-PM01 (spare), three PA003-4 terminals, the contactor, and a soldering iron.

Before the lid goes on

  • Array rewired to 3S1P — three panels in series, one string
  • LM2596 output measured at 5.1 V with the Pi disconnected
  • All three contactor poles chained in series, not paralleled
  • Contactor coil on 24 V, never on the 143 V bus
  • Shunt in the negative return — fat bolts carry current, small screws sense
  • Ferrules crimped on every stranded conductor
  • SPD earth and box earth both landed on the earth bar
  • SPD confirmed DC-rated — an AC unit looks identical and fails badly here
  • Glands on the bottom edge only, tightened onto the cable jacket
  • Breather fitted — a sealed box in the sun will condense inside
  • Bench test passed with the lid open: LCD, relay, shunt config, meter
  • Dashboard reachable over WiFi from your phone
  • Nothing loose that could shift and bridge two terminals

WiFi—no cable; keep the box off metal

Three cables. Everything else lives inside and never gets touched again.

Polycarbonate does not block 2.4 GHz, so the Pi's WiFi works through a closed lid. A metal box would kill it — if you end up using one, the Pi needs an external antenna.
